## The Theory (The What)

A **cast** is an explicit conversion of a value from one type to another. In Kotlin, `as` performs an unsafe cast (throws [ClassCastException]({{ "/en/glossary/class-cast-exception/" | relative_url }}) on failure), while `as?` performs a safe cast (returns `null` on failure). Kotlin's [smart casts]({{ "/en/01-kotlin-core/smart-casts/" | relative_url }}) eliminate the need for manual casting in most situations by having the compiler track type information through control flow.

## The Senior Nuance

- On the JVM, casts are a runtime operation verified by the `checkcast` bytecode instruction. They do not transform the object — they verify its type and either succeed (returning the same reference) or throw.
- Due to generic type erasure, you cannot cast to a parameterized type at runtime (`list as List<String>` compiles with an unchecked warning because the JVM only verifies `List`, not its type parameter). This is why `reified` inline functions exist — they preserve the type parameter at the call site.
- The safe cast `as?` is the Senior default: it composes naturally with the Elvis operator (`value as? String ?: "default"`) and prevents unexpected crashes from incorrect assumptions about runtime types.
