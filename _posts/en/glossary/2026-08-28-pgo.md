## The Theory (The What)

**Profile-Guided Optimization (PGO)** is a compilation technique where the compiler uses data collected from actual [runtime]({{ "/en/glossary/runtime/" | relative_url }}) execution to make better optimization decisions. Instead of guessing which code paths are hot (frequently executed), the compiler knows — because a profiling pass recorded method call frequencies, branch outcomes, and call-site targets during real usage. On Android, ART collects profiles during [JIT compilation]({{ "/en/glossary/jit-compilation/" | relative_url }}) and uses them to guide subsequent [AOT compilation]({{ "/en/glossary/aot-compilation/" | relative_url }}) passes.

## The Senior Nuance

- Android's PGO pipeline works across the entire app lifecycle: (1) ART's JIT compiler profiles method execution during normal use, (2) profiles are saved to disk, (3) during idle device time or on the next app update, ART uses those profiles to [AOT-compile]({{ "/en/glossary/aot-compilation/" | relative_url }}) only the methods that matter. This is why apps "get faster" after a few days of use.
- **Baseline Profiles** (introduced in Android 12) let developers ship a pre-built profile with the APK. This gives ART a head start — critical startup and navigation paths are AOT-compiled on install, without waiting for the device to learn them. Libraries like Jetpack Compose ship their own baseline profiles.
- PGO is the reason why micro-benchmarks on a freshly installed app can be misleading. The first run uses interpreted or minimally JIT-compiled code; real-world performance emerges only after profiles accumulate. Benchmarks should run on warmed-up profiles for meaningful comparisons.
- Google Play can aggregate anonymized profiles from early adopters of an app update and distribute them (via Cloud Profiles) to later users, giving everyone the benefit of PGO without a warm-up period.
