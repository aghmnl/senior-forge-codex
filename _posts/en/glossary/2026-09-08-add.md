## The Theory (The What)

**`add`** is the structural insertion operation of `MutableCollection`. On a [`MutableList`]({{ "/en/glossary/mutable-list/" | relative_url }}) it appends and returns `true`; `add(index, element)` inserts at a position and shifts the rest. On a [`MutableSet`]({{ "/en/glossary/mutable-set/" | relative_url }}) it returns `false` when the element was already present.

```kotlin
// From FollowApp Suite — TasksScreen.kt
// Move one row during a drag: remove and re-insert at the target index
localTasks.add(toIdx, localTasks.removeAt(fromIdx))
```

## The Senior Nuance

- `add` is `O(1)` amortised on an [`ArrayList`]({{ "/en/glossary/arraylist/" | relative_url }}), but `add(0, e)` is `O(n)` — it shifts every element. Building a list in reverse and calling `reversed()` once beats repeated head insertion.
- The `Boolean` return is the underused part. `if (seen.add(id))` deduplicates in a single hash lookup, where [`contains`]({{ "/en/glossary/contains/" | relative_url }}) followed by `add` costs two.
- Calling `add` on a [`List`]({{ "/en/glossary/list/" | relative_url }}) does not compile — and calling it on a frozen result from [`buildList`]({{ "/en/glossary/build-list/" | relative_url }}) via a captured reference throws `UnsupportedOperationException` at [Runtime]({{ "/en/glossary/runtime/" | relative_url }}). Adding to a list while iterating it throws `ConcurrentModificationException`.
