## The Theory (The What)

**`List<out E>`** is Kotlin's ordered, indexed, read-only [collection]({{ "/en/glossary/collections/" | relative_url }}) interface. It declares `size`, `get`/`[]`, [`contains`]({{ "/en/glossary/contains/" | relative_url }}), `iterator` and every [collection operator]({{ "/en/glossary/collection-operators/" | relative_url }}) — but no way to add or remove. `MutableList<E>` is the sub-interface that adds those.

```kotlin
// From FollowApp Suite — GetLabelReferenceCounts.kt
operator fun invoke(tasks: List<Task>, scaleName: String): Map<String, Int>
```

## The Senior Nuance

- A `List` is a [read-only view]({{ "/en/glossary/read-only-view/" | relative_url }}), not an [immutable]({{ "/en/glossary/immutability/" | relative_url }}) object. `listOf()` returns an unmodifiable instance, but a [`MutableList`]({{ "/en/glossary/mutable-list/" | relative_url }}) upcast to `List` is still writable by whoever holds the mutable reference.
- The `out` in `List<out E>` makes it [covariant]({{ "/en/glossary/covariance/" | relative_url }}): `List<Task>` *is* a `List<Any>`. That is only sound because there is no write position — which is exactly why [`MutableList`]({{ "/en/glossary/mutable-list/" | relative_url }}) is [invariant]({{ "/en/glossary/invariance/" | relative_url }}).
- `List` is the default parameter and [return type]({{ "/en/glossary/return-type/" | relative_url }}) for anything crossing a boundary, but it is the wrong choice for repeated membership tests: [`contains`]({{ "/en/glossary/contains/" | relative_url }}) is `O(n)`. Convert to a [`Set`]({{ "/en/glossary/sets/" | relative_url }}) with [`toSet`]({{ "/en/glossary/to-set/" | relative_url }}) when the lookup runs in a loop.
- `List` equality is element-wise **and order-sensitive**, so it participates correctly in [`equals`]({{ "/en/glossary/equals/" | relative_url }}) of a [data class]({{ "/en/01-kotlin-core/data-classes/" | relative_url }}) — and therefore in Compose recomposition decisions.
