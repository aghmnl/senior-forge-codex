## The Theory (The What)

**`toList()`** returns a new read-only [`List`]({{ "/en/glossary/list/" | relative_url }}) containing the elements of the receiver. It is the standard way to take a [defensive copy]({{ "/en/glossary/defensive-copy/" | relative_url }}), and also the terminal operation that materialises a [`Sequence`]({{ "/en/glossary/sequences/" | relative_url }}) or a [`Map`]({{ "/en/glossary/maps/" | relative_url }}) (into a list of `Pair`).

```kotlin
// From FollowApp Suite — PresetMapper.kt
val arr = org.json.JSONArray(state.values.toList())
```

## The Senior Nuance

- `toList()` is what turns a [read-only view]({{ "/en/glossary/read-only-view/" | relative_url }}) into a value. Exposing `_items.toList()` instead of `_items` costs one array copy and removes the whole `ConcurrentModificationException` / stale-render class of bugs.
- The copy is shallow — it duplicates the list structure, not the elements. It protects order and size, nothing more, which is why elements should be [immutable]({{ "/en/glossary/immutability/" | relative_url }}) [data classes]({{ "/en/01-kotlin-core/data-classes/" | relative_url }}).
- On a `Set` it preserves iteration order but drops the `O(1)` [`contains`]({{ "/en/glossary/contains/" | relative_url }}); converting back and forth in a loop is a quiet way to reintroduce an `O(n·m)` scan.
