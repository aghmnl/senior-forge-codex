## The Theory (The What)

A **thread** is the operating system's unit of execution: its own call [stack]({{ "/en/glossary/stack-frame/" | relative_url }}) (~1 MB on Android), its own program counter, scheduled preemptively by the kernel. A process has one [main thread]({{ "/en/glossary/main-thread/" | relative_url }}) and may create more. Threads are expensive to create and to context-switch, and there is a hard practical ceiling of a few thousand per process.

A [coroutine]({{ "/en/glossary/coroutines/" | relative_url }}) is *not* a thread. It is a [state machine]({{ "/en/glossary/state-machine/" | relative_url }}) plus a [Continuation]({{ "/en/glossary/continuation/" | relative_url }}) on the [heap]({{ "/en/glossary/heap/" | relative_url }}) that a [dispatcher]({{ "/en/glossary/dispatcher/" | relative_url }}) runs *on* some thread, and that gives the thread back at every [suspension point]({{ "/en/glossary/suspension-point/" | relative_url }}). One thread can interleave thousands of coroutines.

## The Senior Nuance

- **The unit of blocking is the thread.** A [blocking call]({{ "/en/glossary/blocking-call/" | relative_url }}) parks the whole thread, and every coroutine that was going to run on it waits. That is the entire reason `Dispatchers.IO` is a large [thread pool]({{ "/en/glossary/thread-pool/" | relative_url }}).
- **Thread identity is not stable across suspension.** After a [suspend function]({{ "/en/glossary/suspend-functions/" | relative_url }}) resumes it may be on a different thread of the same pool. `ThreadLocal`, `synchronized` reentrancy and thread-affine SDKs (OpenGL, some native libs) all break silently.
- **Never create raw threads in app code.** `Thread { }.start()` has no cancellation, no error propagation, no [lifecycle]({{ "/en/glossary/lifecycle-aware/" | relative_url }}). Use a scope and a dispatcher.
- **Thread safety is still your problem.** Coroutines on `Default` run in parallel. Shared mutable state needs `Mutex`, atomics, or — preferably — [immutability]({{ "/en/glossary/immutability/" | relative_url }}) and a single writer.
