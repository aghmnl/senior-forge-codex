## The Theory (The What)

A **`Looper`** is the message loop that turns an ordinary [thread]({{ "/en/glossary/thread/" | relative_url }}) into an event-processing thread: it owns a `MessageQueue` and runs forever, dequeuing `Message`s and `Runnable`s and dispatching them to their `Handler`. The [main thread]({{ "/en/glossary/main-thread/" | relative_url }}) has one from process start — every touch event, lifecycle callback, layout pass and frame goes through it as a message.

[`Dispatchers.Main`]({{ "/en/glossary/dispatchers-main/" | relative_url }}) is a `Handler(Looper.getMainLooper())` underneath: resuming a coroutine on Main means posting its [continuation]({{ "/en/glossary/continuation/" | relative_url }}) to that queue.

## The Senior Nuance

- **The queue is why "post to Main" has latency.** A posted message waits behind whatever is already queued — including the current frame's layout and draw. [`Main.immediate`]({{ "/en/glossary/dispatchers-main-immediate/" | relative_url }}) exists to skip the post when you are already on the looper thread.
- **A blocked looper is an ANR.** If the main `Looper` cannot dequeue for ~5 s (input) the system kills the app. [Blocking calls]({{ "/en/glossary/blocking-call/" | relative_url }}) on Main are blocking *the loop*, not just a thread.
- **`HandlerThread`** is a background thread with its own looper — the pre-coroutine way to serialise work on one thread. `Dispatchers.IO.limitedParallelism(1)` is the modern equivalent.
