## The Theory (The What)

**`Job.cancel()`** requests cancellation of a coroutine and of every child in its sub-tree. It is a *request*, not a kill: the [`Job`]({{ "/en/glossary/job/" | relative_url }}) moves to *Cancelling*, and each coroutine in the tree actually stops at its next [suspension point]({{ "/en/glossary/suspension-point/" | relative_url }}), where the [Runtime]({{ "/en/glossary/runtime/" | relative_url }}) throws a [`CancellationException`]({{ "/en/glossary/cancellation-exception/" | relative_url }}). `cancel()` returns immediately; `cancelAndJoin()` also waits for the tree to finish cancelling. Cancelling a [`CoroutineScope`]({{ "/en/glossary/coroutine-scope/" | relative_url }}) cancels its `Job` and therefore everything ever launched on it.

```kotlin
// From FollowApp Suite — TasksViewModel.kt
fun onFormDismissed() {
    subtasksJob?.cancel()
    subtasksJob = null
    // ...
}
```

## The Senior Nuance

- **It only cancels downward.** Cancelling a child does not affect its parent or siblings — that is the difference between *cancellation* (a normal, downward event) and *failure* (an upward one).
- **It is [cooperative]({{ "/en/glossary/cooperative-cancellation/" | relative_url }}).** A CPU loop with no suspension ignores `cancel()` until it checks [`isActive`]({{ "/en/glossary/is-active/" | relative_url }}) or calls [`ensureActive()`]({{ "/en/glossary/ensure-active/" | relative_url }}).
- **Pass a cause when it helps debugging.** `cancel(CancellationException("form dismissed"))` shows up in logs; the plain form does not say why.
- **Cancelling a completed job is harmless.** No need to null-check state before calling it — `subtasksJob?.cancel()` is only guarding against `null`, not against "already done".
