## The Theory (The What)

An **SDK** (*Software Development Kit*) is a vendor's library plus tooling that you integrate to use their service — Google Mobile Ads, Firebase, Play Billing, a payments provider, analytics. On Android it usually arrives as an AAR with an `initialize()` entry point, a [callback]({{ "/en/glossary/callbacks/" | relative_url }})-based API, and sometimes a `ContentProvider` that auto-starts at process launch. From a main-safety point of view an SDK is *opaque code that runs on whatever thread you call it from*.

```kotlin
// From FollowApp Suite — ConsentManager.kt
// MobileAds.initialize does ~700 ms of synchronous SDK bootstrap on
// the caller thread even though it exposes an async callback.
CoroutineScope(SupervisorJob() + Dispatchers.IO).launch {
    MobileAds.initialize(app)
}
```

## The Senior Nuance

- **A callback parameter does not mean the call is asynchronous.** `initialize(context, listener)` can do hundreds of milliseconds of synchronous work before returning and *then* call the listener later. Measure with [profiling]({{ "/en/glossary/profiling/" | relative_url }}); do not trust the signature.
- **SDK init belongs off Main and off the critical path of cold start.** [`Application.onCreate()`]({{ "/en/glossary/application-on-create/" | relative_url }}) is the worst place for it; a background dispatcher after first frame, or lazily on first use, is the right one.
- **Wrap the SDK behind your own interface.** It isolates its threading model, lets you inject a fake in tests, and makes a future SDK swap a one-file change.
